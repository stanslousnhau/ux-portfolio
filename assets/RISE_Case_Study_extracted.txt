Real Income, Steady Earnings
Youth unemployment in South Africa has climbed for a decade from
36.9% in 2015 to 47.4% by Q2 2026. This study explores what happens on
the other side of that statistic: not the job search, but the first few months
of being paid.

STRATEGY


Five design decisions, each replacing a banking convention with a
response to the loop

RISE · UX design case study                                                                                                    9
DESIGN DECISION 1 OF 5


Make the Buffer a first-class financial object, not another savings account

DECISION

The Buffer is surfaced in onboarding and throughout the experience as money
that protects Thabo from falling below zero.

WHY

His measure of progress is not wealth accumulation yet; it is surviving the month
with something left. RISE separates money that protects stability from money
that funds ambition.

REJECTED

Treating the buffer as a savings goal beside a phone or holiday. It would make an
essential safety mechanism compete with aspirations.




     Links to the primary metric: buffer balance above zero.

DESIGN DECISIONS 2 AND 3 OF 5


Treat asking for help as normal banking, and learning as support, not
homework

    Ask RISE: a quick-question channel, not a support ticket
    DECISION           A clear place to ask money questions, with suggested prompts so nobody starts from
                       a blank screen.
    WHY                Embarrassment stops people asking at all (loop point 4), and early on they may lack
                       the terms to search a help centre.
    REJECTED           An FAQ, hidden chatbot or ticketing flow. They optimise support operations, not the
                       psychological barrier.



    Money 101: progressive lessons, not a wall of terms
    DECISION           Short lessons that sit alongside everyday banking and connect to decisions a first-job
                       earner is already making.
    WHY                Education works best near the moment of uncertainty and helps a habit and safety
                       net form (loop point 5).
                                                                                                                Help-seeking and learning live inside the app people
    REJECTED           A literacy hub of articles and definitions. It assumes users know what to learn and                         already use.

DESIGN DECISION 4 OF 5


Start onboarding with context, not compliance

DECISION

A sequence of lightweight questions about employment stage, income and
priorities replaces one dense screen of KYC fields.


WHY

Income alone does not show stability. A new hire, someone on probation and
someone months into a role may earn the same but need very different support.



REJECTED

KYC-first onboarding as the dominant experience. It tells the bank who the
customer is, not what financial transition they are navigating.



     Effect: RISE understands Thabo’s reality before it recommends anything.



RISE · UX design case study                                                     12
DESIGN DECISION 5 OF 5


Warn before the buffer breaks, not after the balance is gone

DECISION

RISE warns when an action is likely to reduce the protected buffer, showing what
may happen next, how much is safe to spend, and how to protect breathing
room.

WHY

It turns an alert into a decision-support moment, using the user’s own context
rather than a fixed number.

REJECTED

A conventional fixed-threshold alert. It reacts too late and ignores upcoming
obligations.




     Where RISE moves beyond a banking app: RISE predicts when breathing room is
     about to disappear.
