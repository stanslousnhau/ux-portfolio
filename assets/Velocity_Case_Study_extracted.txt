The problem and the opportunity

     South African motorists who refuel at Shell service stations in Johannesburg just like Lindiwe have a card-based programme which
     ties their rewards to a card they hardly carry and a points balance they never see, while also lacking an easy digital way to tip fuel
     attendants.


     That's not a minor UX gap. A programme customers can't see or act on daily is a programme that costs money to run without
     earning the repeat visits it's meant to buy. Low visibility isn't just an inconvenience; it's the mechanism that erodes the entire
     business case for the programme.




    We will conduct an exploratory investigation to identify core friction points and define a focused, frictionless MVP digital rewards ecosystem.

Research method
I audited loyalty models across five fuel-retail and adjacent high-frequency categories: supermarket loyalty, banking rewards, and fuel rewards to see
which mechanics correlated with sustained use versus quiet abandonment.

WHY A COMPETITOR ANALYSIS?


    Constraints

    This was self-directed work, without access to real Shell users or stakeholders to validate assumptions through interviews. Rather than skip research, I
    substituted a structured competitive and market audit for direct interviews, a reasonable trade-off in early concept work, but one that means the findings
    are directional, not validated. Programme economics (the cost of cash withdrawals and points-funded tips) were also out of scope and would need
    modelling before any pilot.

What the market audit suggests
    Visibility is the biggest driver of disengagement
    The clearest pattern: low visibility into points and rewards was the single biggest driver of disengagement.



    Invisible value gets abandoned
    Customers who couldn't easily see what they'd earned, or how to use it, simply stopped engaging.               01                                 02


                                                                                                                   Visibility                         Flexibility

    Integrated tipping is a defensible asset
    Tappy already solves cashless tipping on its own, but no rewards programme in the audit lets customers tip from their rewards inside the same journey. That integration,
    not tipping itself, is Velocity's most distinctive lever.


    Flexibility that exists on paper isn't flexibility
    Several programmes offered genuinely flexible redemption: partner catalogues, multiple reward types, tiered perks, but buried those options three or four menus deep, or
    gated them behind thresholds the customer couldn't see until reached.




    Visibility and flexibility must work together, not just exist separately. That combined finding set the direction for the design.

        H1 VISIBILITY                                                Reward visibility increases return engagement.



        H2 CARD-AGNOSTIC                                             Card-agnostic earning matters.



        H3 CASH-OUT                                                  Customers value withdrawing rewards as cash.



        H4 TIPPING                                                   Digital tipping belongs inside the loyalty journey.




   D E C I S I ON R U L E      If a hypothesis fails, that MVP behaviour is cut or reshaped, not defended.

      02 USE IT NOW                                             Redeem during the fuel stop



      03 PAY MY WAY                                             Keep payment card-agnostic



      04 THANK CLEARLY                                          Separate digital tipping from redemption

 Key decisions & visual design system

    01 SEE IT




One unified balance, not separate wallets

Decision: Fuel and in-store purchases feed one visible points balance.
Rejected: Separate fuel-points and shop-points wallets that mirror Shell's business structure.
Many balances are harder to track than one.
Effect: One number reinforces the “one programme” mental model and makes rewards an
active part of the experience.
 DE SI G N DE CI SI O NS




 Key decisions & visual design system


   02 USE IT NOW




Real-time visibility and control over point usage

Decision: Users can redeem or withdraw value close to the moment it is earned.
Rejected: High redemption thresholds, delayed rewards and restricted catalogues. They suit
long-term accumulation but make the benefit feel distant.
Effect: Shortens the distance between behaviour and benefit. Value should not only be earned;
it should be usable.
 DE SI G N DE CI SI O NS




 Key decisions & visual design system


    03 PAY MY WAY




Preserve customer freedom

Decision: Earning stays payment-agnostic; customers choose how they pay.
Rejected: A bank-linked or single-card structure that requires a specific financial product to
qualify.
Effect: Broadens participation and keeps Shell, not a banking partner, at the centre of the loyalty
relationship.
 DE SI G N DE CI SI O NS




 Key decisions & visual design system


    04 THANK CLEARLY




Bring the human service layer into the ecosystem

Decision: Tipping is separate from the fuel transaction, with recipient and amount
made explicit.
Rejected: A purely transactional journey, and gratuity bundled invisibly into the fuel
payment.
Effect: Preserves the human layer of the forecourt while supporting a cashless
future.
