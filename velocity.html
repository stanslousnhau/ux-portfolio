VELOCITY REWARDS MVP · PRODUCT STRATEGY CONCEPT

Make loyalty value visible, flexible and worth returning for.

A self-directed concept for a digital fuel-retail rewards experience. The work explores how customers could see what they earn, choose how to use it, pay with the method they prefer and thank fuel attendants without slowing down a fuel stop.

6loyalty models reviewed in the detailed audit
4MVP behaviours prioritised
1connected fuel-stop journey
THE PROBLEM

Card-bound rewards can be difficult to see and use, while customers lack a simple digital way to tip fuel attendants within the same loyalty experience.

MY PROCESS

Competitor and market audit → behavioural hypotheses → customer needs synthesis → MVP hypotheses → product trade-offs → journey and interface exploration.

MY ROLE & COLLABORATION

Individual, self-directed concept work. I led the research audit, developed hypothesis archetypes, defined the MVP scope and translated the product principles into flows and interface concepts. No Shell stakeholder or customer validation took place.

SUCCESS & STATUS

Repeat engagement is the proposed primary measure, supported by sign-up and redemption signals. These are proposed measures only; the concept has not been piloted and no product impact has been measured.

PRODUCT THINKING · TRADE-OFFS

Flexibility matters, but the fuel stop must stay fast.

The MVP balances customer control with a simple, reliable station experience.

01 · FLEXIBILITY VS. SIMPLICITY

Card-agnostic earning

Supporting more payment methods removes friction, but adds reconciliation and integration complexity. Prioritise a clear earning flow; validate payment compatibility with station systems before expanding.

02 · REDEMPTION VS. VALUE CONTROL

Use rewards now

Immediate redemption makes points feel useful, while unrestricted cash-out can introduce fraud and financial liability. Start with transparent balances and defined redemption rules.

03 · GENEROSITY VS. CHECKOUT FRICTION

Digital tipping

Tipping supports attendants and adds a human layer, but must not slow payment. Keep it optional, quick and clearly confirmed, with the amount and recipient visible.

04 · FEATURE BREADTH VS. ADOPTION

Four core behaviours

Rather than launch a long feature list, focus on seeing rewards, using them, paying with flexibility and thanking an attendant. Defer advanced features until station pilots show they work.

MVP principle: preserve customer choice without adding steps to the moment of purchase.

THE CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE · A GARAGE VISIT

One fuel stop. A smoother rewards experience.

Follow Lindiwe through a familiar visit, from pulling into the garage to thanking the attendant.

01Arriving at the garage

Lindiwe pulls in and chooses a pump, wanting the stop to be quick and straightforward.

02Choosing how to pay

She checks her available payment options and chooses the method that suits her.

03Fuel and earn

The attendant fuels the car. The visit earns rewards without adding another task.

04See the value

Her balance is visible straight away, making the value of loyalty easier to understand.

05Choose a reward

She can decide when and where to use her points, instead of being locked into one option.

06Thank the attendant

Before leaving, she adds an optional digital tip. A small gesture makes the service feel human.

A good fuel stop should feel effortless: clear value, meaningful choice and a simple way to say thank you.
