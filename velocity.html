Case Study 03 · Product Strategy

Velocity Rewards MVP

Designing a petrol-retail rewards experience around the real rhythm of a fuel stop, with visible value, payment freedom, flexible redemption and intentional tipping.

RoleIndividual contributor
DomainPetrol retail · loyalty
ScopeJohannesburg concept pilot
StatusConcept · not shipped

The challenge

Rewards only change behaviour when customers can see and use their value.

Velocity Rewards is a fuel-retail loyalty concept exploring how clearer reward visibility, flexible use and in-journey tipping could make the experience more useful. The work began with a market audit—not direct user research—so the proposed behaviours are hypotheses to validate.

Evidence and boundaries

I used a competitor audit to identify opportunities, not to claim user proof.

The audit compared Sasol Rewards, BP Rewards, Engen 1App, Discovery Vitality Money, eBucks and Tappy. It highlighted three opportunity areas: making reward value visible, reducing friction in redemption, and exploring digital tipping within the fuel journey.

Visibility

Customers need to understand their balance and what it is worth.

Flexibility

Options lose value when thresholds and redemption rules are hard to understand.

Integrated tipping

Digital tipping may fit naturally into the refuelling journey, but needs validation.

The archetypes in the full deck are hypotheses, not interview-derived personas.

Product strategy

Four hypotheses shaped the proposed MVP.

H1 · VISIBILITY

Show reward value clearly

Hypothesis: making value visible will support return engagement.

H2 · PAYMENT FREEDOM

Keep earning card-agnostic

Hypothesis: customers value earning without being tied to one payment card.

H3 · ACCESS

Explore cash-out

Hypothesis: access to cash may make rewards more useful to some customers.

H4 · SERVICE MOMENT

Bring tipping into the journey

Hypothesis: customers may value a simple digital way to thank attendants.

These bets should be tested before committing to a production MVP.

Experience design

Design around the refuelling moment, not a feature catalogue.

The proposed journey connects fuel payment, one visible rewards balance, redemption or withdrawal, and optional tipping. The aim is to reduce switching and keep each action understandable in the context of a station visit.

  • One unified balance for fuel and in-store rewards.
  • Clear, timely redemption and withdrawal choices.
  • Payment flexibility rather than a single-card model.
  • Explicit recipient, amount and confirmation for tipping.
Design rationale

Prioritise the moments that make rewards understandable and usable.

Audit signal

Reward value can be hard to compare

The competitor review highlighted differences in how programmes present value, redemption rules and access. This is a market observation, not direct user evidence.

Product decision

Make value and options visible

The concept brings balances, earning and redemption into a clearer flow, while exploring card-agnostic earning and tipping as separate hypotheses.

Validation needed

Test the value loop

Ask customers to find their balance, explain its value and complete a redemption task. Test tipping with attendants and station operators before assuming it fits operations.

Decision trade-offs

Make rewards flexible while keeping the fuel-stop flow quick.

Option considered

Optimise for one primary reward action

A narrower MVP could be easier to explain and build, but may leave some reward needs unsupported.

Chosen direction

Explore a connected value loop

Balance visibility, earning, redemption and optional tipping in one journey, rather than treating them as unrelated features.

Trade-off to test

Flexibility vs. operational complexity

Cash-out and tipping may add payment, fraud, support and station-workflow considerations. Validate feasibility and customer value before treating them as MVP commitments.

Prototype evaluation plan

  • Customer tasks: find the balance, explain its value, redeem rewards, and understand the confirmation state.
  • Attendant / operator tasks: explain the tipping flow, confirm receipt, and identify exceptions or disputes.
  • Measures: task success, errors, time on task, value comprehension, and operational friction.
  • Decision rule: prioritise features that participants understand and can complete reliably; defer flows that create confusion or unresolved operational risk.
Prototype and next steps

32 screens communicate the concept; they do not prove it works.

The prototype covers onboarding, rewards tracking, redemption, withdrawal, fuel payment, tipping and wallet management. It provides enough of the experience to evaluate the complete value loop rather than isolated screens.

What I would test next

Run task-based usability tests with motorists and attendants, validate comprehension of balances and redemption, test whether tipping fits station operations, and pilot against return rate, service-time impact and programme cost.

Status: product concept · not shipped · hypotheses and outcomes remain unvalidated.
