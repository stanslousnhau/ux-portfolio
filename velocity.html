Case Study 03 · UX Designer

Velocity Rewards MVP

Designing a petrol-retail rewards experience around the real rhythm of a fuel stop.

Individual conceptCompetitor auditProduct strategy + UIJohannesburg pilot concept
01 / Product discovery

Rewards only work when customers can see and use their value.

The concept explores a digital rewards experience for Shell customers in Johannesburg. The opportunity is to make earned value visible, give customers more choice in how they use it and connect rewards to the real service experience at a station.

Customer friction

A card-based programme can make rewards difficult to access when customers do not carry the card or cannot easily see their balance.

Product opportunity

Bring rewards, flexible redemption and digital tipping into one clear journey that supports repeat engagement.

02 / Research and competitor signal

A structured market audit helped identify the gaps worth exploring.

I reviewed loyalty experiences across fuel retail and adjacent high-frequency categories to understand how programmes communicate value, support payment choice and enable redemption.

Sasol Rewards

Familiar earn mechanics and a broad station network.

BP Rewards

Cashback and retail partnerships make value tangible.

Engen 1App

Digital wallet and loyalty features within a partner ecosystem.

eBucks

Deep banking integration and rewards across categories.

Discovery Vitality

Behavioural engagement linked to driving and fuel spend.

Tappy

Demonstrates a lightweight approach to cashless tipping.

What the audit suggested

Visibility and flexibility need to work together.

Customers need to see what they have earned, understand how to use it and reach those choices without navigating unnecessary complexity. Integrating tipping with rewards could create a distinctive service moment, but the economics would need validation.

03 / Behavioural archetypes

Two different customer contexts point to a shared need for control.

Lindiwe · The balance- and value-conscious parent

Needs rewards to be visible and useful in everyday spending. Values clarity, control and practical value.

Jabulani · The convenience-driven customer

Needs a quick, low-friction experience that fits naturally into a fuel stop. Values speed, choice and convenience.

These are hypothesis archetypes used to guide concept decisions, not interview-validated personas.

Payment choice

Do not force one payment method where customers expect flexibility.

Redemption choice

Make it clear where and how earned value can be used.

Generosity choice

Give customers a simple way to direct value towards attendants through tipping.

04 / MVP direction

Prioritise the moments that make rewards tangible.

See the value

Make the rewards balance visible and easy to understand.

Choose how to use it

Make redemption options clear and reduce the effort required to find them.

Pay with flexibility

Support a card-agnostic experience that fits different payment behaviours.

Tip in the same journey

Allow customers to use the experience to recognise attendants, with programme economics and station operations validated before a pilot.

05 / Information architecture and interaction

Keep the core tasks easy to find at the point of need.

The information architecture brings balance visibility, redemption and service actions into a structure customers can understand quickly. This matters in a forecourt context, where attention and time are limited.

06 / Interface design

Bring balance, redemption and tipping into one experience.

The high-fidelity direction makes rewards visible and brings payment freedom and intentional tipping into the same product ecosystem.

07 / Success and next steps

Validate whether the experience creates value for customers and the business.

Engagement

Do customers check their balance and return to use rewards?

Redemption

Can customers understand and complete a redemption without friction?

Service impact

Can tipping be delivered reliably for attendants and operational teams?

Next steps would include customer testing, station staff and operator input, technical feasibility, and modelling the economics of points-funded redemption and tipping before a pilot.

What I learned

A loyalty proposition needs more than points. The value has to be visible, accessible and relevant in the moment customers need it. Product decisions also need to account for operational realities and commercial sustainability.

Scope and evidence

This is a self-directed concept informed by a competitor and market audit. The archetypes are hypotheses, not interview-based personas. The concept and proposed success measures require customer, commercial and station-operations validation.
