Case Study 03 · UX Designer

Velocity Rewards MVP

A fuel-retail loyalty concept exploring how customers can see reward value, choose how to use it and recognise good service with less friction.

RoleIndividual contributor
DomainPetrol retail · loyalty
ScopeJohannesburg concept pilot
EvidenceCompetitor audit and prototype
Read the case study ↓Download full case study ↓
At a glance

Make the value of loyalty easier to see, access and use.

I reviewed fuel-retail and adjacent loyalty experiences, synthesised recurring patterns, and translated the opportunities into a mobile MVP concept covering rewards, fuel payment, redemption and digital tipping.

Problem

Reward value and ways to use it can be difficult to understand.

My contribution

Competitor audit, synthesis, product framing, journey and interface concept.

Success looks like

Customers can understand their balance and complete key tasks confidently, while station workflows remain practical.

Problem

Rewards have limited value if customers cannot easily understand or use them.

A rewards balance only feels valuable when customers understand what it is worth and how to use it. Across a fuel-stop journey, unclear rules or hidden options can turn an apparent benefit into extra effort. I explored how the experience could make value and next steps easier to see.

Visibility

Help customers understand their balance and what it is worth.

Flexibility

Make earning and redemption options easier to understand.

Service moment

Explore whether digital tipping fits naturally into the refuelling experience.

My role and contribution

I connected market evidence to a coherent product direction.

I led the concept work across competitor review, synthesis, experience mapping and interface exploration. I made the findings actionable by turning observed patterns into design principles, prioritised journeys and a prototype for discussion. The case study documents an individual concept, not a shipped product or measured pilot.

Process

Discover, define and design - with each step tied to the problem.

01 · DISCOVER

Understand the loyalty landscape

Reviewed Sasol Rewards, BP Rewards, Engen 1App and adjacent programmes to compare value communication, earning, redemption and service features.

02 · DEFINE

Focus on clarity, choice and service

Synthesised the review into three experience needs: make value visible, give customers understandable choices, and avoid adding friction at the station.

03 · DESIGN

Connect the moments into one experience

Mapped the main tasks, organised the information architecture and explored low- and high-fidelity flows for payment, rewards, redemption and tipping.

04 · COLLABORATION AND INFLUENCE

Make the rationale easy to review

The concept artefacts are structured to support critique with product, engineering and station stakeholders. Their feedback and operational validation remain important next steps; no completed stakeholder testing is claimed here.

01 / RESEARCH SYNTHESIS
Research synthesis

Two archetypes show why control and flexibility matter.

These two archetypes represent different motivations surfaced through the competitor audit. One needs to understand value across everyday spending; the other values the relationship with attendants and wants choice in how to show appreciation. Together they highlight why the experience should not force everyone into one payment or reward behaviour.

Information architecture

Make the core tasks easy to find from one home dashboard.

The information architecture mirrors the physical station journey. From the dashboard, people can move directly to fuel payment, rewards, tipping, or profile and wallet tasks, with each path broken into clear steps.

Reduce friction at the pump

Fuel payment and attendant tipping are distinct, direct paths, helping keep the most time-sensitive tasks easy to reach.

Make value easier to understand

Rewards has a clear home for points balance, redemption and withdrawal instead of hiding them in unrelated flows.

Keep control with the customer

Payment methods and wallet management remain accessible so customers can choose how to pay and use value.

02 / THE CUSTOMER JOURNEY
Proposed user journey

Design around the moments that matter before, during and after a fuel stop.

The journey connects the everyday goal of refuelling with the need to understand rewards and complete tasks quickly.

Arrive and orient

The customer opens Velocity and wants to know what is available without searching through menus.

Need

Immediate clarity on balance and key actions.

Design response

Home dashboard with a visible rewards balance and direct task entry points.

Refuel and pay

The customer wants to complete a familiar station task with minimal delay.

Need

A clear, low-friction payment path.

Design response

Keep fuel payment separate and easy to find from the main dashboard.

Check earned value

The customer wants to understand what has been earned and what it can be used for.

Need

Visible value and understandable redemption choices.

Design response

Bring points into one balance and give rewards a dedicated area.

Choose how to use it

The customer considers redemption, withdrawal or other ways to access value.

Need

Transparent options and clear rules.

Design response

Make redemption and wallet tasks distinct, while treating cash-out as a hypothesis to validate.

Recognise good service

The customer may want to thank an attendant without slowing down the stop.

Need

A quick and intentional tipping interaction.

Design response

A separate tipping flow that clearly identifies the attendant and amount.

Validation opportunity: Test task order, comprehension, time-on-task and whether tipping fits the real station workflow with motorists, attendants and station operators.

03 / COMPETITOR AUDIT
Competitor deep dive · Sasol Rewards

A familiar rewards proposition makes everyday value tangible, but programme rules shape how useful that value feels.

Sasol Rewards is a useful local benchmark because it connects fuel and convenience-store activity to a recognisable rewards proposition.

COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE

What works well

  • Connects rewards to familiar, repeat fuel and convenience purchases.
  • Makes the benefit of participating visible through a points-based proposition.
  • Offers multiple ways to engage with and redeem rewards across the programme.
  • Uses a recognisable fuel-retail brand and station relationship to support trust and routine use.
LIMITATIONS / DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS

Where customers may need more clarity

  • Value is linked to qualifying spend, so customers need to understand what activity earns rewards.
  • Participation and redemption depend on programme terms and eligible locations or transactions.
  • Points are programme value rather than unrestricted cash, which can limit perceived flexibility.
  • Expiry, eligibility and redemption conditions create rules customers must remember.

What Velocity can learn

Show the value and conditions at the moment customers need to make a decision.

Feature to examine

Points balance and redemption: make it easy to see what has been earned and what can be done next.

Design implication

Do not make customers decode programme mechanics to understand their benefit.

This is a qualitative design review, not a customer satisfaction ranking. Confirm current programme terms against the latest official Sasol Rewards information before publication.

COMMON PATTERNS

What the market reinforces

  • Customers need a clear view of reward value.
  • Eligibility and redemption rules influence whether value feels accessible.
  • Mobile tools can connect station activity with ongoing account management.
VELOCITY OPPORTUNITIES

Where the concept can differentiate

  • Bring balance, earning and next actions into one clear view.
  • Make payment and redemption choices explicit, without assuming one preferred method.
  • Explore digital tipping as a distinct service moment, validated with attendants and operators.

05 / DESIGN DECISIONS AND TRADE-OFFS
Key decisions and trade-offs

Make rewards clear and accessible while balancing customer choice with operational needs.

01 · VISIBILITY

Show reward value clearly

Bring balance and value into view so customers can understand what they have earned.

02 · PAYMENT FREEDOM

Explore card-agnostic earning

Consider whether customers should be able to earn without being tied to one payment card.

03 · ACCESS

Explore cash-out

Consider cash access as one possible use of rewards, while checking feasibility and programme economics.

04 · SERVICE MOMENT

Explore digital tipping

Offer a way to thank attendants digitally only if customers, attendants and station operators find it useful and workable.

Trade-off

More choice vs. operational complexity

Flexible redemption may increase usefulness but can add cost, rules and support requirements.

Trade-off

Convenience vs. service flow

Digital tipping should not slow refuelling or create confusion for attendants and customers.

06 / WHAT THE CONCEPT ENABLES
Outcome, success measures and learnings

The concept turns the audit into a reviewable end-to-end experience.

The output is a connected product concept and prototype spanning rewards visibility, fuel payment, redemption and digital tipping. No live-product performance data or user-test results are available in this case study, so impact is framed as measurable success criteria rather than claimed outcomes.

Comprehension

Can customers correctly explain their balance, value and eligibility?

Task success

Can customers find rewards and complete key payment or redemption tasks without assistance?

Operational fit

Can tipping and payment fit the station workflow without adding service friction?

What I learned

The strongest opportunities sit at the intersection of customer value and operational fit. Cash-out and digital tipping could add flexibility, but they also affect programme economics and frontline workflows. These are the areas to investigate before setting MVP priorities.

Next steps

Measure the experience before making claims about impact.

  • Ask motorists to find their balance, explain its value and complete a redemption task.
  • Test earning and redemption rules with customers before prioritising features.
  • Explore tipping with attendants and station operators to check the service flow.
  • Establish a baseline, then track task completion, time-on-task, redemption rate, repeat engagement, tipping adoption, service-time impact and programme cost in a pilot.
