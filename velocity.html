Case Study 03 · UX Designer

Velocity Rewards MVP

A fuel-retail loyalty concept that makes rewards easier to understand and use.

RoleIndividual contributor
DomainPetrol retail · loyalty
ScopeJohannesburg concept pilot
EvidenceCompetitor audit and prototype
Read the case study ↓Download full case study ↓
Summary

Make rewards easier to understand and use at the fuel stop.

I reviewed six loyalty programmes, then designed a concept for reward tracking, redemption, fuel payment, wallet management and digital tipping.

Scope and evidence: Individual concept informed by a six-programme competitor audit and prototype design. It was not tested with customers or piloted in live stations.

Problem

Rewards only matter when customers can use them.

Rewards feel valuable when customers can see what they have and how to use it. I explored ways to make value and next steps clearer during a fuel stop.

Visibility

Help customers understand their balance and what it is worth.

Flexibility

Make earning and redemption options easier to understand.

Service moment

Explore whether digital tipping fits naturally into the refuelling experience.

My role

I translated a competitor review into a product concept and prototype.

I compared Sasol Rewards, BP Rewards, Engen 1App, Discovery Vitality Money, eBucks and Tappy across value visibility, redemption and everyday use.

Process

The journey follows rewards from earning to use.

  • Compare reward visibility, redemption rules and access.
  • Turn gaps into product questions.
  • Map onboarding, payment, rewards, redemption and wallet tasks.
  • Prototype the end-to-end concept.
01 / RESEARCH SYNTHESIS
Research synthesis

Two archetypes show why control and flexibility matter.

Lindiwe wants clear value from everyday spending. Jabulani wants an easy way to recognise attendants. Both need clarity and choice.

Information architecture

Bring key tasks together on the home dashboard.

The dashboard gives direct access to fuel payment, rewards, tipping and wallet tasks, reflecting the main station journey.

Reduce friction at the pump

Keep payment and tipping as separate, quick paths.

Make value easier to understand

Group balance, redemption and withdrawal under Rewards.

Keep control with the customer

Keep payment methods and wallet controls easy to reach.

03 / COMPETITOR AUDIT
Competitor analysis

What the market audit suggests.

Six programmes were reviewed. The audit synthesis below shows the patterns that informed the concept.

Turn competitor patterns into product decisions.

The audit informed these design hypotheses; it did not prove customer demand.

01 · SEE IT

Keep rewards in one visible balance

Decision: show fuel and in-store points in one balance.

Why: the audit highlighted fragmented balances and hard-to-find redemption options.

02 · THANK CLEARLY

Keep tipping separate and explicit

Decision: keep tipping separate, with recipient and amount confirmed.

Why: a separate flow makes tipping intentional rather than hidden in fuel payment.

Trade-off

06 / WHAT THE CONCEPT ENABLES
Outcome / learnings

The prototype connects the proposed value loop.

The prototype connects balance, redemption, fuel payment and digital tipping. Competitor patterns informed the dashboard and proposed reward choices.

Demonstrates: a connected concept, not proven improvements in redemption or engagement.

What I learned

Cash-out and tipping may add value, but need customer, commercial and operational validation before MVP prioritisation.

Next steps

Test clarity, usefulness and operational fit.

  • Ask motorists to find their balance, explain its value and complete a redemption task.
  • Test earning and redemption rules with customers before prioritising features.
  • Explore tipping with attendants and station operators to check the service flow.
  • If piloted, track redemption, return engagement, service-time impact and programme cost.
