Case Study 03 · Product Strategy

Velocity Rewards MVP

A mobile loyalty concept shaped by a competitor review, focused on making rewards easier to understand and use during a fuel stop.

RoleIndividual contributor
DomainPetrol retail · loyalty
ScopeJohannesburg concept pilot
EvidenceCompetitor audit and prototype
Read the case study ↓Download full case study ↓
Summary

Velocity Rewards explores how a fuel-retail loyalty programme could make rewards easier to understand and use.

I reviewed competing loyalty programmes and developed a concept covering reward tracking, redemption, fuel payment, wallet management and digital tipping. The audit helped identify opportunities around reward visibility, flexible use and the service moment at the pump.

Scope and evidence: Competitor audit and concept prototype; no direct user research or live performance results are claimed.

Problem

Rewards have limited value if customers cannot easily understand or use them.

Loyalty experiences can make it difficult to compare reward value, understand redemption rules or see what a balance can do. The concept explored how to make the value clearer during a familiar fuel-stop journey.

Visibility

Help customers understand their balance and what it is worth.

Flexibility

Make earning and redemption options easier to understand.

Service moment

Explore whether digital tipping fits naturally into the refuelling experience.

My role

I translated a competitor review into a product concept and prototype.

I compared Sasol Rewards, BP Rewards, Engen 1App, Discovery Vitality Money, eBucks and Tappy. I used the review to identify opportunity areas and shape the proposed experience.

Process

Map the loyalty value loop from earning to redemption.

  • Audit competitor approaches to reward visibility, redemption rules and access.
  • Identify opportunity areas and turn them into product questions.
  • Map key moments across onboarding, fuel payment, rewards tracking, redemption and wallet management.
  • Build a prototype that communicates the end-to-end concept.
Research synthesis

Two archetypes show why control and flexibility matter.

These hypothesis archetypes represent different motivations surfaced through the competitor audit. One needs to understand value across everyday spending; the other values the relationship with attendants and wants choice in how to show appreciation. Together they highlight why the experience should not force everyone into one payment or reward behaviour.

Evidence note: These are research-informed hypotheses based on the competitor audit, not personas built from direct interviews.

Information architecture

Make the core tasks easy to find from one home dashboard.

The information architecture mirrors the physical station journey. From the dashboard, people can move directly to fuel payment, rewards, tipping, or profile and wallet tasks, with each path broken into clear steps.

Reduce friction at the pump

Fuel payment and attendant tipping are distinct, direct paths, helping keep the most time-sensitive tasks easy to reach.

Make value easier to understand

Rewards has a clear home for points balance, redemption and withdrawal instead of hiding them in unrelated flows.

Keep control with the customer

Payment methods and wallet management remain accessible so customers can choose how to pay and use value.

From wireframes to high-fidelity design

Make reward value visible and make tipping feel clear and intentional.

These low-fidelity Pay It Forward wireframes map the tipping journey from entry, to scanning and confirming the attendant, to choosing a tip amount and payment method. The high-fidelity concept develops two decisions informed by the competitor audit.

01 · SEE IT

Keep rewards in one visible balance

Design decision: Bring fuel and in-store purchases into one visible points balance, so customers have a simple view of what they have earned.

Research rationale: The competitor audit found that low visibility and hard-to-find redemption options weaken perceived value. One balance avoids making customers track separate wallets that reflect internal programme structures.

02 · THANK CLEARLY

Keep tipping separate and explicit

Design decision: Keep tipping separate from the fuel transaction, with the recipient and amount clearly identified.

Research rationale: The audit identified digital tipping as a possible differentiator when integrated into the rewards journey. A distinct flow makes the act of thanking the attendant clear rather than hiding gratuity inside the fuel payment.

Key decisions and trade-offs

Make rewards clear and accessible, while separating assumptions from evidence.

01 · VISIBILITY

Show reward value clearly

Bring balance and value into view so customers can understand what they have earned.

02 · PAYMENT FREEDOM

Explore card-agnostic earning

Consider whether customers should be able to earn without being tied to one payment card.

03 · ACCESS

Explore cash-out

Consider cash access as one possible use of rewards, while checking feasibility and programme economics.

04 · SERVICE MOMENT

Explore digital tipping

Offer a way to thank attendants digitally only if customers, attendants and station operators find it useful and workable.

Trade-off

More choice vs. operational complexity

Flexible redemption may increase usefulness but can add cost, rules and support requirements.

Trade-off

Convenience vs. service flow

Digital tipping should not slow refuelling or create confusion for attendants and customers.

Outcome / learnings

The prototype makes the proposed value loop tangible enough to review.

The concept communicates a connected experience across reward visibility, earning, redemption, withdrawal, fuel payment, tipping and wallet management. The competitor audit informed the direction, but it does not prove customers want every proposed feature.

What I learned

A competitor gap is a useful starting point, not proof of a user need. Features such as cash-out and tipping also affect economics and frontline operations, so they should be tested with customers and service staff before they become MVP commitments.

Next steps

Test comprehension, usefulness and operational fit.

  • Ask motorists to find their balance, explain its value and complete a redemption task.
  • Test earning and redemption rules with customers before prioritising features.
  • Explore tipping with attendants and station operators to check the service flow.
  • If piloted, track redemption, return engagement, service-time impact and programme cost.
