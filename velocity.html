Case Study 03 · UX Design

Velocity Rewards MVP

A self-directed product concept exploring how a fuel-retail loyalty experience could make rewards easier to see, use and redeem while preserving payment choice and bringing optional digital tipping into the journey.

My roleIndividual contributor · Product strategy / UX design
Research approachCompetitive audit · Hypothesis development · MVP design
Project statusSelf-directed concept · Not piloted
Portfolio focusProblem-solving · Evidence · Design decisions
01 · Problem

Card-bound rewards are difficult to see and use. Tipping is missing from the digital journey.

The concept explores a petrol-retail rewards experience built around the real rhythm of a fuel stop. The opportunity is to make rewards value visible and flexible, give customers payment freedom and create an intentional way to thank fuel attendants without slowing the purchase.

VISIBILITY

Make rewards easy to see

Customers should be able to understand what they have earned and what that value can do.

PAYMENT FREEDOM

Remove card dependence

Customers should be able to participate without being restricted to one payment card.

HUMAN SERVICE

Make tipping easier

Offer an optional digital way to thank fuel attendants without adding steps to the fuel transaction.

Product challenge: Payment compatibility with station systems must be validated; unrestricted cash-out can introduce fraud and financial liability; and the fuel stop must remain fast and reliable.
02 · Research method and thinking

A competitor and market audit shaped the early product direction.

This was self-directed concept research. I audited fuel-retail and adjacent high-frequency loyalty models to understand which mechanics made rewards visible, usable and worth returning for. The current slide overview compares three fuel-retail offers directly.

Competitor comparison

  • Sasol Rewards

  • Engen 1App

Research questions

  • Can customers see what they have earned straight away?
  • Can points be turned into something useful?
  • Is there a clear digital way to thank the attendant?
  • Can customers choose when and where to redeem?
Scope: The slide overview documents a market and competitor audit, not customer interviews or a live operational study. The audit provides directional evidence for concept development; it does not validate customer behaviour or programme economics.
03 · Synthesis and insights

Four questions revealed what the loyalty experience needed to make easier.

The market audit organised the opportunity around visibility, usable value, tipping integration and redemption flexibility. The common need identified in the slide overview is control.

05 · Design direction

Design around customer control without slowing down the fuel stop.

The slide overview presents two behavioural archetypes, each pointing to a different expectation of loyalty. Together, they suggest a product direction focused on choice, visible value and a fast experience.

ARCHETYPE 01

Lindiwe Sibanda

The balance- and value-conscious parent. She wants the value of loyalty to be visible and useful across everyday fuel-stop needs.

ARCHETYPE 02

Jabulani Dube

The regular, generous loyalist. He values the relationship with fuel attendants and wants an easier way to express appreciation.

SHARED NEED

Control

Control over payment method, where rewards can be redeemed and how generosity is expressed.

From this direction, the MVP was narrowed to four behaviours: see it, use it now, pay my way and thank clearly. Each behaviour represents a hypothesis to validate, not a proven customer preference.

06 · Solution and design decisions

Four behaviours define the MVP; four decisions shape the experience.

The concept keeps the experience focused on the physical rhythm of a fuel stop. The journey moves from arriving at the garage to choosing how to pay, earning rewards, seeing the value, choosing a reward and optionally thanking the attendant.

01 · SEE IT

One unified balance

Make earned value visible in one place.

02 · USE IT NOW

Flexible redemption

Let customers redeem or access value on their terms, with clear rules.

03 · PAY MY WAY

Card-agnostic earning

Preserve customer choice over how they pay.

04 · THANK CLEARLY

Optional digital tipping

Show the recipient and amount, and keep tipping separate from the fuel transaction.

Product trade-offs: Payment flexibility adds integration and reconciliation complexity. Unrestricted cash-out may create fraud and financial liability. Tipping must not slow checkout. The MVP should preserve customer choice without adding steps to the moment of purchase.

07 · Success and impact

Repeat engagement is the proposed primary measure of success.

The current slide overview defines a success matrix with repeat engagement as the primary measure and sign-up and redemption as supporting signals. These are proposed measures; the concept has not been tested or piloted.

PRIMARYRepeat engagement: whether customers return to use the rewards experience.
SUPPORTINGSign-up: whether customers choose to join the programme.
SUPPORTINGRedemption: whether customers can turn rewards into usable value.
Validation required: The source material explicitly states that the proposed success measures require customer, commercial and station-operations validation. No actual adoption, retention, revenue or service-time results are available.
08 · Lessons learned and next steps

The next test belongs at the station.

The concept sets a direction, but it still needs to be tested against real customer behaviour, station workflows and technical constraints. The most important lesson is that loyalty value must be both visible and usable, while the fuel stop remains fast.

Lessons learned

  • Visible points are not enough if customers cannot easily use them.
  • Payment freedom creates integration and reconciliation challenges.
  • Digital tipping should support the human service experience without slowing payment.
  • Cash-out rules need commercial and fraud-risk consideration.

Next steps

  • Validate the four MVP behaviours with customers.
  • Test payment compatibility with station systems.
  • Assess cash-out rules, fraud exposure and programme economics.
  • Test the digital journey with attendants and station operations.
  • Run a pilot and measure repeat engagement, sign-up, redemption and service-time impact.
Portfolio takeaway: I used competitive research and product thinking to define a focused rewards MVP, make the trade-offs visible and identify what must be validated before a pilot.
