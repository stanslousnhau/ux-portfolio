Problem & process

🚗

Rewards customers can’t see get abandoned.

The problem

Drivers earn points on a card they rarely carry and a balance they never see. Value is tied to one card, and there is no digital way to tip the attendant.

The cost

The programme loses the repeat visits it exists to earn.

CHAPTER 05 OF 08

Design decisions

🚗

Four decisions, with the screens that prove them.

01 · See it

One visible points balance

DecisionFuel and in-store purchases feed one balance, front and centre.
WhyOne number reinforces a “one programme” model.
RejectedSeparate fuel and shop wallets that mirror Shell’s structure.
Rewards home: 1,250 pts, worth up to R125
02 · Use it now

Redeem close to the moment it’s earned

DecisionRedeem or withdraw right after the stop.
WhyIt shortens the distance between behaviour and benefit.
RejectedHigh thresholds, delayed rewards, restricted catalogues.
Redeem: fuel, car wash, coffee or pay with points
03 · Pay my way

Earning stays payment-agnostic

DecisionAny payment method earns, and rewards can pay a tip.
WhyIt broadens participation, and Shell, not a bank, stays central.
RejectedA bank-linked or single-card structure.
Pay It Forward: rewards balance or card
04 · Thank clearly

Tipping is separate, explicit and optional

DecisionRecipient, amount and payment method are confirmed before sending.
WhyIt keeps the human layer while supporting a cashless future.
RejectedA purely transactional journey, or gratuity bundled invisibly.
Review: Thabo Mokoena, R20, rewards balance

Impact & lessons

🚗

Success is repeat visits.

30/60-day returnAgainst a pre-pilot baseline
WhyRepeat engagement tests whether visibility and control create habit: the experience should earn another stop.
SecondaryRedemption. It proves the flow works, not that customers return.
RejectedSign-ups. Curiosity can be bought; it doesn’t prove behaviour changed.

A self-directed concept, so there are no live results. These measures define what impact would look like.

Evidence has a ceiling

An audit without interviews is directional. Saying so early shaped my hypotheses and the kill rule.

Every choice costs a step

Each time I added flexibility, I asked what it did to the moment at the pump.

Metric before pixels

Choosing repeat visits over sign-ups kept the design honest about success.

01InterviewsTest H1–H4
02Task testsBalance, redeem, tip
03Live pilotReturn, service time, cost
04IterateKeep what earns a visit

I used imperfect evidence to make a precise design bet, then defined how I would prove or disprove it.
