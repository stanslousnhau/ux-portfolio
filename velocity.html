Case Study 03 · UX Design

Velocity Rewards MVP

A self-directed product concept exploring how a fuel-retail loyalty experience could make rewards easier to see, use and redeem while preserving payment choice and bringing optional digital tipping into the journey.

My roleIndividual contributor · Product strategy / UX design
Research approachCompetitive audit · Hypothesis development · MVP design
Project statusSelf-directed concept · Not piloted
Portfolio focusProblem-solving · Evidence · Design decisions
3–4 MIN READ · EVIDENCE-LED CASE STUDY
01 · Problem

Make rewards visible and useful without slowing the fuel stop.

Velocity explores a petrol-retail loyalty experience that gives customers clearer value, more payment choice and an intentional way to thank fuel attendants. The challenge is to connect digital rewards to the fast, physical rhythm of refuelling.

01Visible rewards
02Payment choice
03Digital tipping

Constraints: Station-system compatibility, cash-out fraud and liability, and preserving speed at the pump all need validation.

02 · Research method and thinking

A competitor audit shaped the initial product hypothesis.

This was self-directed concept research, not a customer study. I compared Sasol Rewards, BP Rewards and Engen 1App to understand how fuel-retail offers approach earning, payment, redemption and tipping. The audit was organised around four questions that could expose friction in the customer journey.

01

Visibility

Can customers see what they have earned immediately?

02

Usable value

Can points become something useful?

03

Tipping

Can customers thank an attendant digitally?

04

Flexibility

Can customers choose when and where to redeem?

Scope: The audit provides directional market evidence. It does not validate customer behaviour, operational workflows or programme economics.

03 · Synthesis and insights

The common need across the audit was control.

The four audit dimensions pointed to a shared opportunity: customers should understand their rewards, choose how they pay, decide where value is used and control how they express generosity. This became the organising principle for the MVP.

Core synthesis

Reward value is only meaningful when it is visible, accessible and flexible. Tipping can extend the service relationship, but must remain optional and separate from the speed-critical fuel transaction.

04 · Collaboration and influence

This was an individual product-strategy concept.

I framed the problem, completed the competitor audit, developed two hypothesis archetypes and translated the opportunity into MVP behaviours, design decisions and a proposed success model. There is no documented customer or station-stakeholder collaboration in the source material.

ARCHETYPE A

Lindiwe Sibanda

Value-conscious parent seeking visible, useful rewards.

ARCHETYPE B

Jabulani Dube

Regular customer who values the attendant relationship and easy tipping.

Method note: Lindiwe and Jabulani are hypothesis archetypes based on market patterns, not interview-based personas.

05 · Design direction

Design around customer control and a fast service moment.

The two archetypes suggested different motivations but a shared expectation of control. I narrowed the product direction to four MVP behaviours: see it, use it now, pay my way and thank clearly. These are hypotheses to validate, not proven customer preferences.

SEE IT→USE IT NOW→PAY MY WAY→THANK CLEARLY
06 · Solution and design decisions

Four behaviours define the MVP.

The journey moves from arriving at the station to choosing a payment method, earning rewards, seeing the value, redeeming and optionally tipping. The design decisions keep the experience focused while making the trade-offs explicit.

SEE IT

One unified balance

Make earned value easy to understand.

USE IT NOW

Flexible redemption

Give customers clear choices about using value.

PAY MY WAY

Card-agnostic earning

Preserve payment choice, subject to integration feasibility.

THANK CLEARLY

Optional digital tip

Confirm recipient and amount, separate from fuel payment.

Trade-offs: Payment flexibility increases integration and reconciliation complexity; unrestricted cash-out may create fraud exposure and liability; tipping must not add steps at checkout.

07 · Success and impact

Repeat engagement is the proposed primary success measure.

The current slide overview identifies repeat engagement as the primary measure, with sign-up and redemption as supporting signals. The concept has not been tested or piloted, so these are proposed measures rather than results.

PRIMARYRepeat engagement: customers return to use rewards
SUPPORTINGSign-up: customers choose to join
SUPPORTINGRedemption: rewards become usable value
08 · Lessons learned and next steps

The next test belongs at the station.

The concept needs to be tested against customer behaviour, station workflows and technical constraints. Visible points are not enough if customers cannot use them; payment freedom matters only if the system can support it; and digital tipping should strengthen service without slowing the transaction.

  • Validate the four MVP behaviours with motorists.
  • Test the tipping journey with fuel attendants.
  • Assess station-system compatibility and reconciliation.
  • Define cash-out rules and fraud controls.
  • Pilot the experience and measure repeat engagement, redemption and service-time impact.
