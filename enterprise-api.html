Case Study 01 · UX Designer

Enterprise API
Community of Practice

A discovery engagement into the human layer of an enterprise API ecosystem: why teams rebuild, what makes reuse difficult, and how a connected community could help.

UX ResearchStakeholder interviewsAffinity mappingSystems thinking
01 / The challenge

APIs existed across the organisation. Reuse depended on people, relationships and trust.

The discovery focused on understanding what shaped API ownership, collaboration, discovery and reuse across teams. The challenge was not only technical visibility. It was also organisational: how teams found one another, shared knowledge and took responsibility for reusable work.

01

Ownership

Who takes responsibility for an API across its lifecycle, including support and handovers?

02

Incentives and culture

What encourages teams to contribute, share knowledge and reuse existing work?

03

Collaboration and discovery

How do teams coordinate across boundaries and find APIs they can trust and adopt?

02 / Research design

Four investigation territories shaped the research.

The research question was: what makes teams build again instead of reuse?

01 · Ownership

Roles and accountability, ownership handovers, support and maintenance.

02 · Incentives and culture

Team priorities, recognition, incentives and attitudes towards shared work.

03 · Collaboration

Dependencies, handoffs, knowledge exchange and coordination breakdowns.

04 · Discovery and reuse

Search, discoverability, suitability, trust and the choice to reuse or rebuild.

Research goal

Understand the organisational conditions that influence API ownership, collaboration, discovery and reuse.

The territories framed the interview protocol and kept the investigation focused on the behaviours and conditions behind reuse.

03 / My role and approach

From stakeholder conversations to a shared picture of the system.

I worked as part of a seven-person design team, led 9 of 16 stakeholder interviews and contributed to synthesis and ideation.

Prepare the research

Helped shape the protocol around ownership, incentives, collaboration and discovery, using questions designed to surface real examples and working practices.

Conduct stakeholder interviews

Led nine interviews, exploring how teams currently discover, build, maintain and reuse APIs.

Make sense of the evidence

Contributed to affinity mapping, grouping observations and identifying patterns across stakeholder perspectives.

Explore strategic responses

Contributed to ideation that translated research themes into a more connected community proposition.

04 / Synthesis

The opportunity was bigger than creating a searchable directory.

The research pointed towards a human and organisational layer around the API ecosystem: shared ownership, knowledge exchange, visibility and reasons to participate.

Make ownership visible

People need to understand who maintains an API and where to go for support.

Make contribution worthwhile

Sharing needs to fit team incentives and everyday priorities, not rely on goodwill alone.

Support relationships

Reuse is easier when teams can connect, ask questions and learn from one another.

Build trust in discovery

Finding an API is only the start. Teams also need confidence that it is suitable and supported.

05 / Strategic recommendation

Move from a machine model to an organism model.

Rather than treating the Community of Practice as a static catalogue, the recommendation was to think of it as a living system that grows through interaction, identity and shared learning.

The organism metaphor shifts attention from simply storing APIs to enabling people to discover, discuss, improve and reuse them. A sense of belonging can make participation more natural, while self-organisation gives the community room to evolve.

The design direction

From directory to community.

Help people find APIs, but also help them find owners, exchange knowledge, learn about API capabilities and build relationships around shared practice.

Identity and belonging

Give members a reason to see themselves as part of a shared practice.

Interaction and learning

Support questions, discussion and learning about APIs, including how they work and where they can be used.

Self-organisation

Allow useful activity and knowledge to emerge from the community, supported by clear governance.

Trust and consistency

Balance autonomy with standards, ownership and dependable information.

Scope and evidence

Research conducted within a seven-person design team. I led 9 of 16 stakeholder interviews and contributed to synthesis and ideation. Client details are anonymised under NDA.
