Enterprise API Community of Practice

A discovery engagement into the human layer of an enterprise API ecosystem: why teams rebuild, what makes reuse difficult, and how a connected community could help.

UX ResearchStakeholder interviewsAffinity mappingService and systems thinking
Original research presentation rebuilt as a responsive website case study.
3–4 MIN READ · EVIDENCE-LED CASE STUDY
01 · Problem

The API Marketplace existed. Confidence in reuse did not.

The Marketplace was live, but leadership lacked evidence about why teams struggled to discover, trust and reuse APIs. This discovery engagement investigated the human and organisational conditions behind that gap and informed a proposed API Community of Practice.

Research question What makes teams build again instead of finding, trusting and reusing what already exists?

02 · Research method and thinking

A structured interview process made the system visible.

I designed the protocol around four objectives: understand business and technical roles; identify barriers to collaboration and reuse; assess qualitative maturity; and explore readiness for a Community of Practice. I led 9 of 16 interviews, each 30–45 minutes, recorded with consent.

01 / DESIGN

Role-specific protocol

Five-part interview flow, scenario prompts, consent and a separate executive track.

02 / LISTEN

Cross-functional voices

Architects, subject-matter experts, business analysts, developers, UX, API leads and executives.

03 / SYNTHESISE

Bottom-up analysis

Transcript fragments clustered by behaviour, then prioritised by frequency, intensity and business impact.

03 · Synthesis and insights

Six themes showed that the barriers ran deeper than the platform.

I helped turn interview observations into 20 insights and six recurring themes. The synthesis looked for patterns across roles rather than treating individual comments as findings.

01

Discovery is people-dependent

Teams relied on personal networks and tribal knowledge.

02

Duplication is normalised

Building again felt safer than assessing someone else’s API.

03

Habits are entrenched

Established ways of working outweighed the case for change.

04

Ownership is fragmented

Unclear accountability weakened confidence in shared assets.

05

Governance feels like a gate

Processes were associated with delay and workarounds.

06

Reuse has little payoff

Delivery incentives rewarded new builds over shared contribution.

Central insight: API reuse was not simply a search problem. Trust, ownership, habits and incentives shaped behaviour. Detailed board content is withheld under NDA.

04 · Collaboration and influence

The findings became actionable through shared interpretation.

I worked within a seven-person design team and with bank stakeholders. I owned the interview protocol, led 9 interviews and facilitated affinity mapping and ideation. The group reviewed patterns, challenged interpretations and explored what each finding meant for a Community of Practice.

MY CONTRIBUTION

Research continuity

Connected protocol design, interviews, synthesis and facilitated ideation.

SHARED WORK

Co-interpretation

Used a shared affinity board and behaviour-change questions to move from evidence to ideas.

Influence: The direction was shaped with stakeholders, not simply handed over as a design-team recommendation.

05 · Design direction

Shift from a platform-centred fix to an adaptive ecosystem.

The evidence challenged the assumption that better tooling alone would improve reuse. The direction reframed the Community of Practice as an operating model: make ownership visible, lower the perceived risk of reuse and recognise contribution as valuable work.

PEOPLE

Make expertise discoverable

Connect APIs to owners and trusted contacts.

TRUST

Make reuse feel safer

Reduce uncertainty when finding and assessing shared capabilities.

INCENTIVES

Reward contribution

Recognise reuse as part of delivery, not extra work.

06 · Solution and design decisions

The output was a strategic direction, not a new interface.

Three recommendations translated the research into practical design implications: make API owners discoverable; reduce the effort and uncertainty of evaluating shared APIs; and recognise reuse and contribution. The broader machine-to-organism reframe proposed distributed ownership, self-organising parts and continuous adaptation.

Decision principle

Design the conditions for reuse before adding another layer of process. The recommendations informed the proposed Community of Practice; they are not presented as implemented changes.

07 · Success and impact

Success was a stronger evidence base and a clearer leadership conversation.

The documented outcome was a traceable set of themes, a qualitative maturity read and shared language for discussing the operating model. The machine-to-organism metaphor helped communicate strategic trade-offs.

6Evidence-led themes
1Operating-model reframe

Measurement boundary: No post-project change in discovery time, reuse rate, duplicate API creation or developer satisfaction was documented. These are proposed future measures, not achieved outcomes.

08 · Lessons learned and next steps

Validate the operating model before reaching for more tooling.

The strongest lesson was continuity: staying close from protocol through synthesis protected the link between what people said and what the team recommended. Enterprise problems can appear technical while being shaped by culture, incentives and behaviour.

  • Test the proposed model with future Community of Practice participants.
  • Clarify API ownership, named contacts and contribution expectations.
  • Baseline discovery time, reuse and duplicate creation.
  • Check whether governance and recognition changes make reuse easier in practice.

My takeaway: Research created value by reframing the problem and giving leadership a grounded direction for change.
