PROBLEM STATEMENT
A leading South African financial institution had a live API Marketplace but no evidence of whether teams trusted, found or reused what was on it. Leadership suspected collaboration, discoverability and reuse were breaking down, yet could not say where or why. Without evidence, the proposed Community of Practice risked being designed on assumptions.

How might we design a Community of Practice around how people actually discover, trust and reuse APIs?

Why interviews, why these people, why this way

Interviews, not a survey

WHYThe question was “where and why”. That needs stories, not ratings.

16 people, 7 groups

WHYOne ecosystem, many seats. A theme counted only if several groups raised it.

Think-aloud and scenarios

WHYScenarios show what people do, not what they say.

“Diagnosis, not audit”

WHYOwnership is sensitive. Naming the intent and observers unlocked candour.

A separate executive track

WHYSame themes, different altitude: risk and investment.

Four objectives first

WHYEvery question traced to a goal. Nothing was asked “just in case”.

CHAPTER 06 OF 08Solution & design decisions
  • Three decisions, tied to evidence
  • The ideation session

Three decisions, each tied to evidence

1

People over platforms

EVIDENCETeams trusted people they knew over what the platform listed.

DECISIONFindable owners and named contacts beside every API.

REJECTEDA platform-only fix such as better search or listings.

WHYTrust here is personal, so discovery has to be too.

2

Make reuse the easy default

EVIDENCEBuilding fresh felt safer than finding and vetting.

DECISIONLower the cost of trusting a shared API.

REJECTEDMore governance gates, which already felt like delay.

WHYRebuilding won out because trusting cost more.

3

Reward reuse

EVIDENCEDelivery pressure and recognition favoured shipping new.

DECISIONRecognise reuse and contribution as valued work.

REJECTEDTreating reuse as another compliance step.

WHYEffort without credit will not change behaviour.
