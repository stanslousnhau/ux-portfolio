Enterprise API Ecosystem Research

I researched why teams struggled to find and reuse existing APIs, then synthesised the findings into practical recommendations for improving discovery and trust.

RoleUX Researcher
Team7 designers
ContextTier-1 African bank · NDA
EvidenceStakeholder interviews and synthesis

Summary

Research revealed that API reuse was a people and operating-model problem, not just a search problem.

Within a large African banking environment, I helped investigate why teams rebuilt capabilities instead of reusing APIs already available. I designed the interview protocol, independently led 9 of 16 stakeholder interviews, contributed to synthesis and helped translate the findings into six cross-cutting themes and recommendations.

Scope and evidence: Qualitative discovery under NDA; findings informed recommendations, not a measured production outcome.

Problem

Why were teams rebuilding APIs instead of reusing existing ones?

The API Marketplace existed, but people still relied on personal networks to find capabilities. Unclear ownership, uncertainty about support and delivery pressure made reuse feel risky or slower than rebuilding.

Process

Move from recent behaviour to shared patterns.

I designed a stakeholder interview protocol that moved from context to recent examples, delivery friction, culture and future needs. Scenario prompts encouraged participants to describe what they actually did, rather than rely on abstract opinions. I then helped facilitate affinity mapping and ideation.

  • Frame the research question around API discovery and reuse.
  • Interview stakeholders about real workflows and workarounds.
  • Group observations into patterns and prioritise themes by frequency, intensity and business impact.
Key decisions and trade-offs

Improve discovery while making reuse feel safe.

Decision

Look beyond search

Better search could help people find APIs, but would not resolve uncertainty about ownership, support or suitability.

Recommendation

Make ownership visible

Show clear owner/contact details and enough context for consumers to judge whether an API fits their needs.

Trade-off

Useful detail vs. maintenance

Metadata helps only if it stays accurate. The minimum useful information and its ownership process would need to be agreed with API teams.

Outcome / learnings

The research reframed the challenge from a tool issue to a system of behaviours.

The work produced a qualitative evidence base, six themes and a strategic direction for leadership. It highlighted how discoverability, trust, ownership and incentives interact. The recommendations remained recommendations; their effect on API reuse was not measured.

What I learned

In enterprise UX research, a visible usability issue can be the surface of a broader organisational problem. Interviewing about real examples and connecting individual workarounds to shared incentives helped avoid designing a search-only fix.

Next steps

Test the recommendations against real API-consumer tasks.

  • Co-design the ownership and operating model with API consumers and owners.
  • Test whether users can find a suitable API, identify its owner and judge whether it fits.
  • Measure task success, time to discovery, confidence and metadata freshness.
  • Track reuse and duplicated effort only after an intervention is implemented.
