Enterprise API Ecosystem Research

I investigated why teams rebuilt existing capabilities and what would make API reuse easier.

Team7 designers
ContextA leading financial services organisation in South Africa · NDA
EvidenceStakeholder interviews and synthesis
Read the case study ↓
01 / OVERVIEW
Summary

API reuse was a people and operating-model challenge, not just a search problem.

At a leading South African financial services organisation, I designed the interview protocol, led 9 of 16 stakeholder interviews and helped synthesise six themes into strategic recommendations.

Scope and evidence: Qualitative discovery under NDA; findings informed recommendations, not a measured production outcome.

02 / THE PROBLEM
Problem

Why were teams rebuilding APIs instead of reusing existing ones?

The API Marketplace existed, but teams still relied on colleagues to find APIs. They also needed clear ownership, support and confidence that reuse would not slow delivery. When those conditions were unclear, rebuilding felt safer.

03 / PROBLEM FRAMING
Issue tree

Four areas shaped the research.

The issue tree organised the inquiry into four areas. These were questions to investigate, not confirmed findings.

THE CENTRAL RESEARCH QUESTION

What makes teams build again instead of reuse?

Understanding the organisational conditions behind API ownership, collaboration, discovery and reuse.

WHAT THE RESEARCH NEEDED TO REVEAL

These research territories helped frame recommendations for a more connected API community.

RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS

API reuse may be shaped by ownership, incentives, team culture and cross-team collaboration as well as technology.

Research goal: Investigate how organisational structures, team behaviours and collaboration practices influence API ownership, discovery and reuse, and identify opportunities to improve the enterprise API ecosystem.

05 / RESEARCH APPROACH
Protocol design

The interview protocol moved from daily work to future change.

I began with participants’ roles and daily work, then explored behaviours, barriers and possible improvements. Scenario questions encouraged concrete examples rather than idealised answers.

01 · Opening and context

Understand roles and context

Focus: role, API responsibilities and current discovery habits.

Objective: understand different teams’ experiences of the API ecosystem.

Why: establish context and trust before exploring problems.

02 · Current state

Find friction in daily work

Focus: discovery barriers, duplicated work and reasons APIs may not be trusted.

Objective: identify barriers to collaboration, discovery and reuse.

Why: recent examples reveal actual behaviours and workarounds.

03 · Delivery behaviour

Explore responses to change

Focus: scenarios involving API changes, ownership, governance and handoffs.

Objective: understand practical barriers and organisational readiness.

Why: scenarios show how teams respond when responsibilities or dependencies change.

04 · Culture and readiness

Understand what enables sharing

Focus: knowledge-sharing, incentives and interest in a Community of Practice.

Objective: identify conditions that could support participation.

Why: reuse also depends on motivation, trust and relationships across teams.

05 · Future state and executive track

Explore strategic change

Focus: desired future, improvement priorities, leadership and risks.

Objective: inform the CoP’s design, operating model and governance.

Why: practitioners described daily work; executives explored system-level change.

Why this sequence mattered

This sequence moved from context to behaviour, then to culture and future change without introducing a preferred solution too early.

06 / SYNTHESIS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Key decisions and trade-offs

Keep the platform. Build a community around it.

The recommendation was to strengthen, not replace, the API Marketplace. The machine-to-organism metaphor shifted the focus from a fixed tool to a community that learns, adapts and shares responsibility.

Strategic reframe

From machine to organism

A machine relies on tools and central control. An organism depends on trust, shared learning and teams adapting together.

Community and identity

Create belonging, not just a directory

Create a Community of Practice where API practitioners build shared identity, exchange experience and learn across teams.

Self-organising capability

Enable interaction and learning

Enable member-led questions, peer support, shared patterns and ways to find expertise and learn about APIs.

The trade-off

Keep clear standards and accountability, while giving members room to organise. Too much control limits participation; too little can weaken trust and quality.

Affinity mapping

Make sense of the system together

I facilitated affinity mapping, grouping observations by behaviour to reveal recurring patterns and build a shared view of the ecosystem.

Ideation session

Turn the insight into a different operating model

In ideation, the team used the emerging themes to explore community, shared identity, self-organisation and learning beyond a tool-led response.

07 / OUTCOME AND LEARNINGS
Outcome / learnings

Make API reuse a shared practice.

The six themes pointed to a community that supports the existing platform through shared identity, peer learning and responsibility.

What I learned

The design challenge was bigger than improving a platform: it was about how people connect, learn and take ownership.

08 / NEXT STEPS
Next steps

Build a learning, self-organising community.

  • Co-design a shared purpose and participation model with API consumers, owners, architects, developers and business stakeholders.
  • Support self-organisation with lightweight roles, contribution patterns and clear guardrails.
  • Enable peer questions, reusable examples, knowledge exchange and access to expertise.
  • Connect the Marketplace to API context, ownership and peer support.
  • Pilot with a focused group. Track participation, time to find expertise, reuse confidence and duplicated effort.

Evidence boundary: This work was conducted under NDA. The case study shares the research approach and strategic direction without exposing the underlying research deck or sensitive organisational detail.
