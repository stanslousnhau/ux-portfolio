THE FIRST PAYCHEQUE · THABO'S STORY

Getting the job was only the beginning.

Follow Thabo from the effort of finding work to the financial pressure that arrives with his first steady income.

01Applying for jobs

Thabo spends evenings searching and applying for entry-level roles, hoping one opportunity will open a door.

02Printing his CV

He pays to use an internet cafe and print several copies of his CV, carefully checking each page before leaving.

03Buying data

With an interview approaching, he has to buy mobile data so he can join the Microsoft Teams meeting from home.

04The Teams interview

He finds a quiet spot, checks his connection and joins the Teams call, trying to make a strong impression despite the pressure.

05Job offer after many rejections

After many rejections and unanswered applications, the call finally comes through: Thabo has been offered the job. The relief and excitement are enormous. After so many setbacks, he finally has a chance to begin a new chapter.

06More responsibilities, less certainty

His salary now has to stretch across rent, transport, food, family support and other commitments. He is proud to be working, but overwhelmed and unsure how to organise his money or what he can safely spend.

The transition into work brings new responsibilities before financial confidence has had time to catch up. RISE is designed to help Thabo understand his commitments, protect a Buffer and make his next money decision with more confidence.

PRODUCT THINKING · TRADE-OFFS

Protecting the Buffer without taking control away.

The design had to reduce financial uncertainty without creating another restrictive banking experience.

CHOICE 01

Safety vs. spending freedom

Trade-off: Locking money away could protect savings, but would make the Buffer feel inaccessible.

Decision: Make the Buffer visible and intentional, while keeping the user in control of transfers.

CHOICE 02

Early warnings vs. alert fatigue

Trade-off: Frequent alerts may catch problems early, but can create anxiety and be ignored.

Decision: Focus on a projected shortfall and pair the warning with a practical next action.

CHOICE 03

Guidance vs. independence

Trade-off: More advice can support confidence, but risks overwhelming or judging the user.

Decision: Offer contextual, optional guidance rather than prescriptive financial rules.

WHAT STAYS OUT OF THE MVP

Complex automation

Automated transfers and highly personalised recommendations are deferred until trust, income patterns and user preferences can be tested.

Design principle: make the risk visible, make the next step understandable, and leave the decision with the person.
