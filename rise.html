Case Study 02 · UX Designer

RISE: Real Income, Steady Earnings

A financial wellbeing concept exploring how young South Africans could better understand upcoming expenses and protect money until payday.

RoleUX Designer
DomainFintech · financial wellbeing
Location contextSouth Africa
EvidenceSecondary research and composite persona
Read the case study ↓Download full case study ↓
01 / OVERVIEW
Summary

RISE explores how a clearer view of upcoming expenses could help young earners plan between paydays.

I developed a financial wellbeing concept for young South Africans balancing daily costs, family obligations and personal goals. Secondary research and a composite persona informed the design direction, including a projected buffer, contextual guidance and low-pressure next steps.

Scope and evidence: Concept based on secondary research and a composite persona; no original user interviews or usability results are claimed.

02 / FINANCIAL WELLBEING PROBLEM
Problem

A current balance does not always show what is safe to spend.

Someone can see money in an account and still face pressure when transport, food, family support and other obligations arrive before the next payday. The design question was how to make upcoming pressure easier to anticipate without adding shame or presenting estimates as certainty.

03 / TARGET USERS AND CIRCUMSTANCES
Who this concept is for

Young first-job earners balancing independence with responsibility to others.

The primary design focus is a young South African who has recently started earning but has little room for unexpected costs. The composite persona, Thabo, is 23, lives in Johannesburg and earns R6,800 per month. This is a research-informed composite, not a real interview participant.

FINANCIAL CIRCUMSTANCES

Little margin for error

Transport, food, debit orders and family contributions compete for the same income. A surprise deduction can put the rest of the month under pressure.

BEHAVIOURS AND NEEDS

Protect what is left

He checks his balance after payday and before larger purchases, mentally separates money for essentials and wants to know what will remain after upcoming costs.

EMOTIONAL CONTEXT

Support without shame

He wants to help at home and build financial progress, but jargon and fear of being judged can make it harder to ask questions or seek guidance.

Scope: Persona details are used to make the design problem concrete. They should be validated with real first-job earners before being treated as representative of all young South Africans.

04 / RESEARCH AND DISCOVERY
Discovery process

Triangulate secondary research, then turn the pattern into a design hypothesis.

This was a secondary-research-led concept project. I compared signals about financial stress, digital financial confidence and young people's outlook, then used synthesis to focus the product question. I did not conduct primary interviews or usability testing for this concept.

01 · REVIEW

Look beyond budgeting habits

Reviewed the cited Debt Rescue Money Stress Tracker, SA Social Media Landscape report and Old Mutual Youth Generational Wealth Survey as three different signals related to financial stress, digital financial skills and financial outlook.

02 · SYNTHESISE

Frame the core need

Used the composite persona to connect irregular pressure moments, upcoming deductions, family obligations and the need to protect a buffer.

03 · FORM A HYPOTHESIS

Interrupt the monthly loop

Proposed that showing what is coming, not only what is currently available, could help people make a more informed decision before the buffer is depleted.

Research caveat: These sources suggest a structural problem but do not prove what any individual user needs. The original case study notes that self-reported skills and outlook data would be strengthened by income and cost-of-living data.

05 / KEY INSIGHTS AND DESIGN OPPORTUNITIES
Research synthesis

The opportunity is not just to show a balance, but to help someone understand what that balance needs to cover.

INSIGHT 01

Today’s balance hides tomorrow’s pressure

People can see money in an account without seeing how upcoming costs may change what is safe to spend.

Opportunity: Bring known upcoming expenses and a projected buffer into the same view.

INSIGHT 02

The buffer represents breathing room

Money left after essentials is not only a savings target. It can protect transport, food and other needs when plans change.

Opportunity: Give the Buffer a distinct, understandable role and show whether it may fall below a safe amount.

INSIGHT 03

Shame can prevent people from asking

Judgemental language and financial jargon may make support feel inaccessible at the moment it is needed.

Opportunity: Offer plain-language, private, low-pressure guidance and quick questions.

INSIGHT 04

A forecast must be honest about uncertainty

Missing or changing expenses can make a projection wrong, so a confident-looking number could mislead.

Opportunity: Show assumptions, allow corrections and describe forecasts as estimates, not promises.

06 / USER JOURNEY AND PROPOSED SOLUTION
Proposed target-state journey

Help the user move from uncertainty to a more informed next step.

This is a proposed journey based on the research synthesis, not an observed or validated user journey.

01 · ONBOARD

Share the basics

The user adds payday timing and known recurring obligations, with clear explanations of why each detail is requested.

Need: understand the setup and feel in control
02 · CHECK IN

See the Buffer

The dashboard shows the current balance, upcoming expenses and an estimated amount that may remain after those costs.

Need: make the numbers meaningful
03 · SPOT RISK

Understand a possible shortfall

If a planned spend may push the Buffer below its safe amount, RISE explains the possible impact in plain language.

Need: notice risk early, without panic
04 · CHOOSE

Explore an action

The user reviews options, adjusts an expense or corrects an assumption. RISE supports the decision rather than making it for them.

Need: retain agency and flexibility
05 · LEARN OR ASK

Get support without judgement

A quick question or contextual lesson helps the user understand a decision at the moment it matters.

Need: feel safe asking for help
06 · REVIEW

Check progress before payday

The user revisits the Buffer and sees whether money remains. Future testing would check if the forecast was understandable and useful.

Need: build confidence over time
Proposed solution

RISE combines context-first onboarding, a projected Buffer, early warnings, a quick-question channel and progressive financial learning. Together, these features aim to make upcoming pressure visible and support a practical next step while keeping the user in control.

07 / WIREFRAMES AND INTERFACE DESIGN
From sketches to high-fidelity design

Make the buffer visible, then help people act before it runs low.

The early sketches explored three connected moments: checking the home dashboard, recognising a possible buffer shortfall, and asking RISE a money question. The high-fidelity concept develops two research-led decisions from that direction.

01 · PROTECT THE BUFFER

Give the Buffer a clear role

Design decision: Surface the Buffer as money that protects day-to-day stability, rather than treating it as another aspirational savings goal.

Research rationale: The case study frames the first-paycheck challenge around surviving the month with money left, while the composite persona highlights the need to protect financial breathing room.

02 · WARN BEFORE THE BUFFER BREAKS

Turn an alert into decision support

Design decision: Warn when an action may reduce the protected buffer, and show what could happen next and how much may be safe to spend.

Research rationale: The case study identifies fixed-threshold alerts as too late because they ignore upcoming obligations. The proposed warning uses the person’s context instead of a single balance threshold.

Key decisions and trade-offs

Make financial breathing room visible without making forecasts look guaranteed.

01 · SEE AHEAD

Projected buffer

Show known upcoming expenses alongside the current balance to make a potential shortfall easier to spot.

02 · MAKE IT TANGIBLE

Buffer as a goal

Give the money left for essentials and unexpected costs a clear role in the experience.

03 · ASK SAFELY

Quick-question channel

Explore a low-friction way to ask money questions without shame.

04 · LEARN IN CONTEXT

Progressive lessons

Offer guidance when it can support a real decision, rather than overwhelm users at the start.

05 · START WITH CONTEXT

Context-first onboarding

Understand the person’s situation before suggesting actions.

Trade-off

Helpful prediction vs. false precision

Missing or changing expenses can make a forecast misleading. Make assumptions visible, allow correction and avoid presenting estimates as guarantees.

Trade-off

Guidance vs. judgement

Prompts should help people consider options without implying that financial pressure is a personal failure.

Outcome / learnings

The concept connects financial visibility with a practical next step.

The design exploration established a direction for a projected buffer, contextual learning and low-pressure support. It is a set of proposed screens and product decisions, not evidence that the experience improved financial outcomes.

What I learned

Financial product design needs to balance clarity with humility. A forecast can support planning, but only if users understand its assumptions and can correct it. Tone matters too: guidance should preserve dignity and choice.

08 / MEASURING SUCCESS
Success metrics

Measure whether the Buffer survives until payday, not whether people simply open the app.

The case study defines the north-star outcome as having money left in the Buffer on the day before the next payslip arrives. This matters because sign-ups and app opens can rise without improving a person's financial position. The Buffer metric checks whether the experience may be helping interrupt the cycle of reaching payday with nothing left.

Primary · Buffer balance > R0Is there still something in the Buffer the day before the next payslip?
Primary · Safety net survivesDoes protected money remain through the month, rather than disappearing before payday?
Secondary · Payday app opensUseful as a signal of habit, but not proof of improved financial wellbeing.
Secondary · Notification attentionCheck whether calm, contextual warnings are noticed, then pair this with Buffer outcomes.

These are proposed measures from the case study, not observed results. No numeric targets are claimed before testing with real first-job earners.

09 / LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEPS
Limitations and next steps

Check whether people understand, trust and can act on the projection.

LIMITATION 01

Secondary research only

No original interviews or usability sessions were conducted, so the persona, pain points and proposed journey remain hypotheses.

LIMITATION 02

Forecast quality depends on inputs

Missing expenses, irregular income and changing obligations can make the projected Buffer inaccurate. The interface must make assumptions visible and editable.

LIMITATION 03

Not built or validated

RISE is a design exploration. No shipped product, measured impact or proven improvement in financial wellbeing is claimed.

  • Conduct interviews with first-job earners to check the problem framing and assumptions.
  • Test whether people can explain the projected buffer and identify a likely shortfall.
  • Measure comprehension, task completion, confidence and perceived judgement.
  • Revise the forecast and labels if users mistake estimates for guaranteed balances.
