Case Study 02 · Product Design

RISE: Real Income, Steady Earnings

A financial wellbeing concept exploring how young South Africans could better understand upcoming expenses and protect money until payday.

RoleIndividual contributor · UX Designer
DomainFintech · financial wellbeing
Location contextSouth Africa
EvidenceSecondary research and composite persona
Read the case study ↓Download full case study ↓
Summary

RISE explores how a clearer view of upcoming expenses could help young earners plan between paydays.

I developed a financial wellbeing concept for young South Africans balancing daily costs, family obligations and personal goals. Secondary research and a composite persona informed the design direction, including a projected buffer, contextual guidance and low-pressure next steps.

Scope and evidence: Concept based on secondary research and a composite persona; no original user interviews or usability results are claimed.

Problem

A current balance does not always show what is safe to spend.

Someone can see money in an account and still face pressure when transport, food, family support and other obligations arrive before the next payday. The design question was how to make upcoming pressure easier to anticipate without adding shame or presenting estimates as certainty.

My role

I shaped the concept from research framing through screen-level design decisions.

I reviewed secondary research, developed a composite persona to focus the problem, framed the user need and explored a set of product features for financial visibility, contextual learning and accessible support.

Process

Translate the concern into a focused design direction.

  • Review secondary research signals around financial stress and uncertainty.
  • Use a composite persona to frame a clear user need: protect a buffer without feeling judged.
  • Explore screens that connect upcoming obligations to a useful next action.
  • Consider how onboarding, learning and support could fit into the user’s context.
Key decisions and trade-offs

Make financial breathing room visible without making forecasts look guaranteed.

01 · SEE AHEAD

Projected buffer

Show known upcoming expenses alongside the current balance to make a potential shortfall easier to spot.

02 · MAKE IT TANGIBLE

Buffer as a goal

Give the money left for essentials and unexpected costs a clear role in the experience.

03 · ASK SAFELY

Quick-question channel

Explore a low-friction way to ask money questions without shame.

04 · LEARN IN CONTEXT

Progressive lessons

Offer guidance when it can support a real decision, rather than overwhelm users at the start.

05 · START WITH CONTEXT

Context-first onboarding

Understand the person’s situation before suggesting actions.

Trade-off

Helpful prediction vs. false precision

Missing or changing expenses can make a forecast misleading. Make assumptions visible, allow correction and avoid presenting estimates as guarantees.

Trade-off

Guidance vs. judgement

Prompts should help people consider options without implying that financial pressure is a personal failure.

Outcome / learnings

The concept connects financial visibility with a practical next step.

The design exploration established a direction for a projected buffer, contextual learning and low-pressure support. It is a set of proposed screens and product decisions, not evidence that the experience improved financial outcomes.

What I learned

Financial product design needs to balance clarity with humility. A forecast can support planning, but only if users understand its assumptions and can correct it. Tone matters too: guidance should preserve dignity and choice.

Next steps

Check whether people understand, trust and can act on the projection.

  • Conduct interviews with first-job earners to check the problem framing and assumptions.
  • Test whether people can explain the projected buffer and identify a likely shortfall.
  • Measure comprehension, task completion, confidence and perceived judgement.
  • Revise the forecast and labels if users mistake estimates for guaranteed balances.
