Case Study 02 · UX Designer

RISE

Real Income, Steady Earnings. Exploring how a first-job earner can build financial breathing room after the first payslip.

Individual conceptSecondary researchFinancial wellbeingProduct strategy + UI
01 / Problem

The first salary does not always create financial breathing room.

For a young person entering employment, a regular payslip can bring new responsibilities without removing the pressure of everyday expenses. The design question became: how might a banking experience help someone understand what is safe to spend while protecting a small financial buffer?

Research goal

Explore how first-job earners can build and protect a small financial safety net between paydays.

The concept focuses on the moment after income arrives, when competing expenses make it difficult to know what is genuinely available.

02 / Context and synthesis

The problem is a repeating pressure loop, not a single budgeting mistake.

01 · PAYDAYIncome arrives and commitments compete for attention.02 · OBLIGATIONSFixed costs and family responsibilities take priority.03 · UNCERTAINTYRemaining money is difficult to interpret.04 · PRESSUREUnexpected costs can consume the little that remains.

The design opportunity is to make the remaining balance meaningful and help people act before a shortfall becomes urgent.

Composite persona: Thabo

Thabo represents a first-job earner navigating a new income, existing responsibilities and uncertainty about whether money will last until the next payslip.

Core need

“I need to know that I will still be okay before my next payday.” This is a design framing for the concept, not a direct research quote.

03 / Design strategy

Make the Buffer a clear financial object.

Rather than showing only an account balance, RISE separates money that is available to spend from money the user wants to protect as a safety net.

01

Protect the Buffer

Give the Buffer a clear role and make its status visible alongside safe-to-spend money.

02

Warn before it breaks

Use an early warning to explain a projected shortfall and support a decision, rather than simply reporting a low balance.

03

Make support approachable

Make asking for help and learning about money feel like normal parts of the banking experience.

04 / Low-fidelity exploration

Start with the structure of the decision.

The original hand-drawn sketches explore the dashboard, an early warning and a way to ask RISE for support. They helped work through the information hierarchy before moving into interface detail.

05 / Interface design

Turn the Buffer into something people can understand and act on.

01 · Protect the Buffer

Make the protected amount distinct from the money available for everyday spending. The dashboard should answer a practical question quickly: what can I spend without putting my safety net at risk?

02 · Warn before the Buffer breaks

Show the projected pressure early and pair the warning with context and possible next steps. The alert should support a decision, not create panic.

06 / Measuring success

A safety net that survives the month matters more than an app open.

The primary success measure is whether a user still has money in their Buffer the day before their next payslip arrives.

Primary
Buffer > 0

Whether a safety net survived until the next payday.

Secondary
Payday use

Whether people engage with the experience around the moment income arrives.

Guardrail
No added stress

Check whether alerts and guidance feel useful rather than judgemental or anxiety-inducing.

These are proposed measures for validation, not measured product results.

What I learned

Financial wellbeing is not only about tracking spending. It is also about helping people interpret their choices, protect what matters and feel more confident between paydays. A useful product should make the next decision clearer without making the user feel judged.

Scope and evidence

This is a self-directed design exploration informed by secondary research. Thabo is a composite persona. The statistics and assumptions require validation with first-job earners. The concept has not been built or shipped.
