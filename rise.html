RISE: Real Income, Steady Earnings

A financial wellbeing concept helping young earners see upcoming costs and protect money until payday.

RoleIndividual contributor · UX Designer
DomainFintech · financial wellbeing
Location contextSouth Africa
EvidenceSecondary research and composite persona
Read the case study ↓Download full case study ↓
01 / OVERVIEW
Summary

RISE helps young earners understand what their money still needs to cover.

I used secondary research and a composite persona to shape a concept around a projected Buffer, timely warnings and practical guidance.

Scope and evidence: Concept informed by three secondary sources and a composite persona. No primary interviews or usability testing were conducted, so the proposed benefits remain hypotheses to test.

02 / FINANCIAL WELLBEING PROBLEM
Problem

A current balance does not always show what is safe to spend.

A balance shows what is available now, not what is already committed. RISE brings upcoming costs into view without presenting forecasts as guarantees.

03 / TARGET USERS AND CIRCUMSTANCES
Who this concept is for

Young first-job earners balancing independence with responsibility to others.

The composite persona, Thabo, represents a 23-year-old first-job earner in Johannesburg, earning R6,800 a month. He is a research-informed profile, not an interview participant.

FINANCIAL CIRCUMSTANCES

Little margin for error

Transport, food, debit orders and family support compete for the same income.

BEHAVIOURS AND NEEDS

Protect what is left

He checks his balance before spending and wants to know what upcoming costs will leave him.

EMOTIONAL CONTEXT

Support without shame

He wants to support his family and make progress, but financial jargon can make asking for help difficult.

04 / RESEARCH AND DISCOVERY
Discovery process

Three research sources framed the money challenge.

I reviewed three sources on money stress, digital financial confidence and young people’s financial outlook. Together, they framed the question: how can someone tell what is safe to spend before payday? No primary interviews or usability tests were conducted.

05 / RESEARCH GOAL
How might we

Research goal: make everyday money decisions clearer.

RESEARCH GOAL 01 · INCOME ALLOCATION

How might we help young earners understand what they can safely spend, save and set aside for upcoming expenses?

Research focus: understand how young earners divide income, manage uncertainty and balance spending, saving and upcoming costs.

06 / KEY INSIGHTS AND DESIGN OPPORTUNITIES
Research synthesis

Show what a balance still needs to cover.

INSIGHT 01

Today’s balance hides tomorrow’s pressure

Interpretation: a balance does not explain what that money still needs to cover.

Opportunity: show upcoming expenses beside a projected Buffer.

INSIGHT 02

The buffer represents breathing room

The Buffer is breathing room for essentials when plans change, not simply spare cash or a savings target.

Opportunity: give the Buffer a clear role and flag when it may run low.

INSIGHT 03

Shame can prevent people from asking

Jargon or judgement can make people less likely to ask for help.

Opportunity: offer private, plain-language support.

INSIGHT 04

A forecast must be honest about uncertainty

Missing or changing expenses can make projections inaccurate.

Opportunity: show assumptions and let people correct estimates.

08 / WIREFRAMES AND INTERFACE DESIGN
From sketches to high-fidelity design

Make the Buffer visible before it runs low.

I focused the interface on two moments: checking what is safe to spend and spotting a shortfall before payday.

01 · PROTECT THE BUFFER

Give the Buffer a clear role

Decision: present the Buffer as protection for everyday essentials, not another savings target.

Why: the persona needs financial breathing room between paydays.

02 · WARN BEFORE THE BUFFER BREAKS

Turn an alert into decision support

Decision: warn users before spending may reduce the Buffer, then show the likely impact and options.

Why: a fixed balance alert ignores upcoming bills. A contextual warning considers what is still due.

Key decisions and trade-offs

Show financial breathing room without false certainty.

01 · SEE AHEAD

Projected buffer

Show upcoming expenses beside the current balance.

02 · MAKE IT TANGIBLE

Buffer as a goal

Make protected money visible and understandable.

03 · ASK SAFELY

Quick-question channel

Offer a simple, judgement-free way to ask questions.

04 · LEARN IN CONTEXT

Progressive lessons

Introduce guidance when it can help with a decision.

05 · START WITH CONTEXT

Context-first onboarding

Understand the user’s context before giving suggestions.

Trade-off

Helpful prediction vs. false precision

Show forecast assumptions and let users correct them; estimates are not guarantees.

Trade-off

Guidance vs. judgement

Use supportive language without blaming the user.

Outcome / learnings

Connect financial visibility to a practical next step.

The concept combines a projected Buffer, contextual learning and low-pressure support. These are design hypotheses, not proven outcomes.

What I learned

Forecasts need visible assumptions and user control. Guidance should preserve dignity and choice.

09 / MEASURING SUCCESS
Success metrics

Measure whether money remains in the Buffer before payday.

North-star metric: money remaining in the Buffer the day before payday. This is closer to the intended outcome than sign-ups or app opens, but must be tested with real users and their circumstances.
