Case Study 02 · Product Design

RISE: Real Income, Steady Earnings

Designing financial breathing room for young South Africans navigating the first months after a first paycheck.

RoleIndividual contributor · UX Designer
DomainFintech · financial wellbeing
Location contextSouth Africa
StatusDesign exploration · not shipped
Read the case study ↓Download full case study ↓
The challenge

A first salary can bring new pressure, not immediate financial security.

RISE explores how young South Africans entering work can manage obligations and everyday costs while keeping enough money to reach the next payday. The opportunity is not to claim an app can solve structural financial pressure, but to help people see what is coming and make safer decisions.

This is a concept based on secondary research and a composite persona—not a validated intervention or a live product.

Research and framing

I translated several signals into a focused design hypothesis.

Financial stress

Secondary research suggested that anxiety and uncertainty can shape decisions after payday.

Low forward visibility

The concept responds to a gap between seeing a current balance and understanding what will remain after upcoming expenses.

Clear evidence limits

The figures used are contextual secondary research. They are not original participant findings and need validation with first-job earners.

User need

Help me protect a buffer without feeling judged.

The composite persona, Thabo, represents a first-job earner balancing personal goals and family obligations. The key design question became: how might the experience help someone anticipate pressure and make a small, timely adjustment before their buffer disappears?

  • Show what is likely to remain after known expenses.
  • Make the next useful action understandable and low-pressure.
  • Support learning in context instead of overwhelming people upfront.
Design decisions

Five ideas support one goal: keeping the buffer visible and actionable.

01 · SEE AHEAD

Predictive buffer warning

Surface a likely shortfall before it becomes an urgent problem.

02 · MAKE IT TANGIBLE

Buffer as a first-class object

Make financial breathing room a visible goal, not just a leftover number.

03 · ASK SAFELY

Quick-question channel

Offer a low-friction way to ask money questions without shame.

04 · LEARN IN CONTEXT

Progressive money lessons

Introduce guidance when it can support a real decision.

05 · START WITH CONTEXT

Context-first onboarding

Understand the person’s situation before recommending actions.

Design rationale

Turn the financial concern into a visible, non-judgemental action.

Signal from secondary research

Knowing today’s balance is not the same as knowing what is safe to spend

Upcoming obligations can make a current balance misleading. This is a design hypothesis drawn from secondary research, not a finding from original user interviews.

Design decision

Show a projected buffer

Bring known upcoming expenses into the picture so users can see a potential shortfall before payday pressure peaks.

What the screen must prove

Clarity without blame

In usability testing, check whether people understand the projection, trust its assumptions and can choose a useful next step without feeling judged.

Decision trade-offs

Make the future visible without pretending the forecast is certain.

Option considered

Show the current balance

Simple and familiar, but it can hide upcoming obligations and create false confidence about what is safe to spend.

Chosen direction

Show a projected buffer

Bring known expenses into view so people can anticipate pressure and decide what to do next.

Trade-off to test

Helpful prediction vs. false precision

A projection can mislead when expenses or income are missing. Make assumptions visible, allow correction, and avoid presenting estimates as guarantees.

Usability test plan

  • Participants: first-job earners with varied levels of budgeting confidence.
  • Tasks: explain the projected buffer, identify an upcoming shortfall, and choose a next action.
  • Measures: correct comprehension, task completion, confidence, perceived judgement, and whether assumptions are understood.
  • Decision rule: revise labels or forecast detail if participants mistake the estimate for a guaranteed amount or cannot explain how it was calculated.
Validation plan

The next step is to test whether the concept genuinely helps.

The screens illustrate a design direction, not proof of impact. I would test whether first-job earners understand the projected buffer, trust the warnings, and can choose a useful next action without feeling blamed or overwhelmed.

Measures to evaluate

Comprehension of upcoming expenses, confidence in the next action, task completion, perceived judgement, and whether people can accurately explain their remaining buffer.

Status: design exploration · not shipped · user validation still required.
